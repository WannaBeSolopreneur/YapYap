Privacy

What you read and say never leaves your Mac.

You don’t have to take our word for it. Here is what the code does, and how to check.

Two one-time downloads.

The natural Kokoro voices, about 340 MB from GitHub, fetched in the background the first time you open YapYap (you can remove them in Settings). And on macOS 26 or later, Apple’s speech model, which macOS fetches itself. Text, audio and screenshots are never uploaded.

Kokoro.swift

Screenshots stay in memory.

Each read takes one still image of the window or box you chose. macOS’s Vision framework turns it into text, then the image is thrown away.

Capture.swift

Your voice stays on your Mac.

Dictation uses Apple’s on-device speech recognition, and the optional tidy-up uses Apple Intelligence on your Mac. The mic is on only while you dictate: while you hold the shortcut, or in hands-free mode until you stop it. Audio is never saved.

Dictation.swift

No account. Anonymous stats you can switch off.

YapYap sends anonymous counts, like how many words were read, through TelemetryDeck. Never what you read or say, or which app it was in. Turn it off in Settings → Privacy.

Telemetry.swift

Check it yourself.

  • Search the code. YapYap’s own network code fits on three lines. The only other connection is TelemetryDeck’s anonymous stats.
  • Turn off Wi‑Fi. Once the voice and speech model are installed, reading and dictation work exactly the same.
  • Watch the traffic. Run a network monitor such as LuLu while YapYap reads or listens. Turn stats off and nothing else goes out.

Questions and answers.

What is YapYap?

YapYap is a free, open-source Mac app that does two things. It reads any text on your screen aloud, highlighting each word as it’s spoken, and it types what you say into any app. Reading and dictation both run on your Mac, with no account.

How do I get my Mac to read text aloud with YapYap?

Press ⌥A, then drag a box around the text you want to hear. YapYap finds the words, even in screenshots, PDFs and photos, and reads them aloud while a highlight follows each word on the original page. Press Esc to stop.

How does dictation work?

Hold ⌥S and talk. Your words appear live in a small panel at the bottom of the screen. Let go and YapYap types the text wherever your cursor is, in any app. For longer dictation, tap ⌥S instead: it keeps listening until you tap it again or click ✓. It can drop filler words and tidy the text with Apple Intelligence, on your Mac.

Does YapYap send my voice or screen anywhere?

No. Text on screen is recognised with Apple’s Vision framework, dictation uses Apple’s on-device speech recognition, and the voices run locally. YapYap only sends anonymous usage counts, never content, and you can turn that off in Settings.

Does YapYap work offline?

Yes. Once the Kokoro voices and macOS’s speech model are downloaded, reading and dictation both work with Wi‑Fi turned off. Until the voices arrive, or if you remove them, YapYap reads with the voices built into macOS.

Which Macs does YapYap run on?

YapYap needs macOS 15 Sequoia or later on a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or newer). It asks for Screen Recording permission to read, and Microphone and Accessibility permission to dictate.

What languages does YapYap support?

The natural Kokoro voices speak US and UK English. Dictation uses Apple’s on-device speech recognition for your Mac’s language where Apple supports it. YapYap is designed and tested in English.

How is YapYap different from Speechify or Wispr Flow?

YapYap is free and open source, with no subscription. It handles both reading aloud and dictation in one app, and it does both on your Mac instead of sending your text or voice to a server.

How do I install YapYap?

Download YapYap, open the file, and drag YapYap onto Applications. It’s signed and notarized by Apple, and it keeps itself up to date. You can also build it from the source on GitHub.
